The Problems I Solve

If any of these sound familiar, we should talk.

01 · First-Time Outsourcing
"We have never outsourced a project — let alone to a team on another continent."

No internal process, no reference points, no network. The risk feels unquantifiable.

I make outsourcing structured and transparent — with clear milestones, contracts, and governance that let you stay in control from Europe.
02 · Failed Asia Attempt
"We tried Asia before. It did not go well."

Wrong supplier, quality failure, missed delivery. Once burned, the instinct is to avoid it — even when the economics demand it.

I know which manufacturers to trust and which to avoid. My network is built on 15+ years of on-the-ground relationships, not directories.
03 · No Engineering Team
"We don't have an engineering team — but our business depends on delivering a product."

The commercial opportunity is real. The technical capability is not there. Hiring takes too long and the risk of a wrong hire is high.

I bring both the engineering leadership and the Asian manufacturing network to deliver the product — without requiring you to build a team first.
04 · Cost Reduction Strategy
"We need to reduce costs through outsourcing — but have no idea how to structure it."

Margins are under pressure. What to outsource, to whom, and in what sequence is unclear.

I diagnose where cost is actually created, build the outsourcing strategy around that, and execute it with clear owners and milestones.
05 · IP & Confidentiality
"We want to outsource R&D — but cannot afford to lose control of our IP."

Especially critical in medtech and defense. Fear of IP leakage to Asian manufacturers often prevents companies from acting at all.

I structure outsourcing with IP segregation, NDA frameworks, and contractual protections validated across regulated industries.
06 · No Single Owner
"Everyone is involved — but no one owns the result."

Projects span R&D, quality, supply chain, and manufacturing. Each team does its part. But nobody drives the outcome end to end.

I own the outcome — one accountable person from kickoff to delivery, regardless of how many teams are involved.
